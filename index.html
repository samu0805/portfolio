P.01 0–24 VAC SPWM Controller (STM32) STM32 SPWM Isolation +
PROBLEMOBJECTIVEDESIGNHARDWARESOFTWAREPCBTESTRESULTS
Problem
Most bench AC sources for testing low-voltage loads are bulky, expensive, or lack fine amplitude control. A compact, digitally controlled source was needed for lab and prototype validation work.
Objective
Design a solid-state controller producing a variable AC output from 0–24 V, with adjustable frequency and clean sinusoidal shaping via SPWM.
Design
Full-bridge topology driven by an STM32 timer-generated SPWM pattern, LC output filter to reconstruct the sine wave, and galvanic isolation between control and power stages.
Hardware
Gate driver stage, H-bridge MOSFETs, gate isolation transformers, LC filter sized for the target frequency band, and protection circuitry for overcurrent conditions.
Software
STM32 firmware generating a lookup-table-based sine reference, timer PWM peripheral configured for the switching frequency, and a simple UART interface for amplitude/frequency commands.
PCB
Two-stage board: isolated low-power control section and high-current power section, with careful trace width and creepage/clearance for the isolation barrier. [schematic + layout images go here]
Test
Oscilloscope verification of SPWM switching, output waveform THD measurement after filtering, and thermal checks under sustained load. [scope captures go here]
Results & Learnings
Achieved a clean, adjustable 0–24 VAC output with low distortion; refined filter sizing and gate isolation approach through iteration. [add final numbers: THD %, efficiency, settling time]

04 — Who's Behind This
About

I'm an electronics engineering student focused on power electronics and hardware design — the layer between control theory and the physical circuit that has to actually survive current, heat, and noise.

My work moves along one line: Electronics → Embedded Systems → Power Electronics → Energy Systems. Each project on this site is a step further into that specialization, from firmware and PCB layout toward converters, BMS design, and grid-facing power systems.

I'm currently exploring how that specialization applies to real energy infrastructure — battery storage, power quality, and the regulatory frameworks shaping how power electronics gets deployed at scale.
